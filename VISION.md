# MassHelp Vision

> No one who meets the rule should lose their health insurance over paperwork.

## Tldr

Starting January 1, 2027, many MassHealth members aged 19 to 64 in the expansion group will have to prove, month after month, that they work, volunteer, train, study, or qualify for an exemption. They will also have to renew every six months instead of once a year.

Most of the people this affects already meet the rule. The danger is not that they fail the work test. The danger is that they fail the paperwork test.

MassHelp exists to close that gap. We help eligible people prove what is already true, on time, in a form MassHealth will accept, so they keep their coverage.

## Problem

In July 2025, Congress passed H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OB3). Massachusetts must put its Medicaid changes into effect for MassHealth, which covers about 1.87 million residents.

Under the new rules, an adult in the expansion group keeps coverage by showing, for a given month, at least one of the following:

- 80 hours of work, volunteering, job training, or a combination
- Enrollment in school at least half-time
- At least $580 in earnings that month
- An exemption, such as being the parent of a child 13 or younger, being pregnant, being medically frail, or being in addiction treatment

Exempt people are still at risk. They often have to prove that they qualify for the exemption.

The state itself estimates that about 175,000 people could lose coverage. History tells us why:

- **Arkansas, 2018.** More than 95% of the people subject to the rule already met it or should have been exempt. Even so, 18,000 people lost coverage in 7 months. Many had never heard of the rule. They missed letters, misunderstood the process, or lacked the right documents.
- **Medicaid unwinding, 2023 to 2024.** Of the 25 million people dropped nationwide, 69% were cut for missing paperwork, not for being ineligible.
- **No employment gain.** Harvard researchers found no increase in work in Arkansas. The only measurable result was more uninsured people.

Masshelp aims to address the weak spot in the paperwork step.

## Who is most at risk

The state's automated data often cannot confirm eligibility for these groups, so the burden of proof falls on them:

- **Gig workers.** App-based income does not show up in state wage data.
- **Students.** Enrollment status may not be shared automatically.
- **People with disabilities.** Medical frailty must often be documented.
- **People with limited English.** Notices and forms are hard to understand.
- **People without reliable internet or stable housing.** Mail goes to old addresses, and online portals are out of reach.

## Maria's story

Maria drives for DoorDash about 25 hours a week and earns about $900 a month. That already beats the $580 threshold. She qualifies. Here is how she still loses coverage:

1. MassHealth cannot see gig income in its wage data, so it cannot confirm her automatically.
2. A blue envelope goes to her old apartment, or sits unopened because it looks like junk mail.
3. If she does open it, the form asks for "two recent pay stubs." She has none, only a DoorDash app screen.
4. Thirty days pass, and her coverage ends.
5. She finds out at the pharmacy when her prescription is not covered.
6. Before, coverage could reach back 3 months to pay an emergency room bill. Starting in January, it reaches back only 1 month, so that visit becomes her debt.

Maria followed the rule the whole time. She lost coverage over paperwork.

**MassHelp is built so that Maria's story ends differently.**

## Why it matters

Losing coverage is costly for everyone involved:

- **For people.** Treatment stops, including insulin, mental health medication, and cancer care. Medical debt follows, now that retroactive coverage is shorter.
- **For providers.** Hospitals and clinics absorb unpaid bills.
- **For the state.** MassHealth processes the same people again when they reapply, paying twice for an outcome no one wanted.

## What MassHelp does

MassHelp is a guide that walks a MassHealth member from "I got a letter" (or "I didn't know about any of this") to "my coverage is safe."

1. **Know if you are affected.** A short, plain-language check tells a member whether the rule applies to them, and whether they likely qualify through work, earnings, school, training, volunteering, or an exemption.
2. **Understand the notice.** Members can describe or photograph a MassHealth letter and get a clear explanation of what it asks for, what the deadline is, and what happens if they miss it, in their own language.
3. **Turn real life into acceptable proof.** MassHelp helps people gather the documents they actually have, such as gig app earnings screens, bank deposits, school enrollment letters, volunteer logs, or a provider's note, and organize them into what MassHealth asks for.
4. **Never miss a deadline.** Reminders by text, email, or phone for monthly reporting, six-month renewals, and response windows, so nothing depends on a single envelope.
5. **Submit with confidence.** Step-by-step help completing and sending the response through the channel that works for the member, whether online, by mail, by fax, or in person.
6. **Recover fast if coverage is lost.** If coverage ends, MassHelp explains how to reapply or appeal right away, to keep the gap and the debt as small as possible.
7. **Connect to people.** When a case is complicated, MassHelp hands off to navigators, community health workers, legal aid, and health center staff, with the member's information already organized.

## Principles

- **Assume the person qualifies.**
- **Plain language first.** Every message should make sense to someone reading it on a phone, in a hurry, in their second language.
- **Meet people where they are.** Mobile-first and text-friendly, with low-bandwidth and offline-friendly options. No app download required. Multilingual supported, English prioritized for the demo.
- **Accessible by default.** Usable with screen readers, large text, and simple navigation for people with disabilities.
- **Privacy is non-negotiable.** Health, income, and immigration-adjacent information is sensitive. Collect the minimum, explain why, keep it secure, and never sell or share it without consent.
- **Neutral and accurate.** Information must match current MassHealth rules and be updated as guidance changes. When we are unsure, we say so and point to an official source or a human.
- **Augment, do not replace.** MassHelp supports MassHealth, navigators, and community organizations. It does not make eligibility decisions or give legal advice.

## What MassHelp is not

- Not an advocacy campaign for or against work requirements
- Not a substitute for MassHealth's official determination
- Not a legal service, though it connects people to legal aid
- Not a data-collection product

## Measuring success

We succeed when eligible people keep their coverage. We will track:

- Members who keep coverage after a verification or renewal request
- Responses submitted before the deadline
- Time from notice to completed response
- Coverage losses for procedural reasons among people we helped
- Time to reinstatement for anyone who does lose coverage
- Member understanding and confidence, in their own words

The long-term goal is simple: in Massachusetts, the number of people who meet the rule and still lose coverage should be as close to zero as possible.

## The bottom line

The new rule is mostly a paperwork test. The people most likely to fail it are the ones who already pass the real test. MassHelp makes sure they can prove it.
