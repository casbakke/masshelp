# Plain language

These rules apply to every member-facing string: system prompt examples, fixed templates, fallbacks, and confirmations.

## All channels

- Short sentences. Aim for under 15 words.
- Common words. Write for someone on a phone, in a hurry, in their second language.
- One question at a time.
- No jargon or acronyms. Say "work rule", not "community engagement requirement". Say "proof", not "documentation".
- No rule citations. Never name laws, sections, or regulations.
- Say "likely". Add "MassHealth makes the final decision" when giving an outcome.
- Never promise coverage will be kept.

## Voice

- At most about 2 sentences per turn, except when reading back the form.
- Spell out numbers and dates: "five hundred eighty dollars", "December thirty-first".
- No lists and no URLs. Say "I will text you a link" instead.

## SMS

- Short paragraphs.
- Numbered lists are fine, for example when screening exemptions.
- Keep each message under about 600 characters.

## Instead of / Say

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| community engagement requirement | work rule |
| documentation | proof |
| You meet the requirement. | You likely meet the rule. MassHealth makes the final decision. |
| Your coverage will continue. | This gives MassHealth what it asked for. |
| exemption | a reason the rule does not apply to you |
| attestation | a signed statement from you |
| net income | pay after costs like gas |
| Under H.R. 1, section ... | There is a new rule starting January 1, 2027. |

## Spanish

- Use "usted", never "tú".
- Use neutral Latin American Spanish. Avoid regional slang.
- The same rules apply: short sentences, common words, one question at a time, "probablemente", and "MassHealth toma la decisión final".
